# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Privacy-conscious music listeners who prefer owning their library over streaming. Currently a solo build; expanding to public release. Users open Listenwell at their desk or laptop to play files they've uploaded themselves. They're not casual: they've chosen to leave streaming platforms deliberately, which means they care about control, privacy, and having their collection on their own terms. They use it in focused listening sessions and background-music contexts equally.

## Product Purpose

Listenwell is a local music player for people who don't want to rent their music library. No subscription, no data harvesting, no algorithm, no engagement loops. Users upload their own audio files and build playlists that belong to them. Success means the player disappears into the background and the music comes forward: fast, responsive, and entirely under the listener's control.

## Brand Personality

Clean, futuristic, modular. The tone is confident and quiet: not minimal-for-minimalism's-sake, but precise. Nothing decorative that doesn't earn its presence. Futuristic here means exact, not sci-fi flashy. Modular means the UI feels like composed, discrete parts rather than one continuous surface.

## Anti-references

- **Spotify**: rounded, organic, algorithm-forward, discovery-obsessed. Heavy brand presence. Feels like a service's product, not the user's space.
- **Apple Music**: editorial warmth, editorial layout, genre suggestions, "For You" curation. The interface constantly nudges what you should listen to next.
- Both: any interface that feels like it's trying to retain your attention or guide your behavior. Listenwell has no opinion about what you should play.

## Design Principles

1. **Ownership over service.** The interface belongs to the listener. No recommendations, no trending, no suggestions. Every surface should reinforce that this is their library, not a curated feed.
2. **Precision over pageantry.** Futuristic means exact, not flashy. Controls feel tight and intentional. Visual weight goes to what's active: the current track, the queue, the waveform. Everything else recedes.
3. **Modular composition.** Panels, screens, and controls should feel like discrete, composable parts. Nothing bleeds into everything else. The queue is its own thing. The song list is its own thing. Clarity through containment.
4. **The file is the source of truth.** Metadata, album art, and playback all trace back to what the user uploaded. The UI reflects the library as-is, without editorializing.
5. **Silence the algorithm.** No dark patterns, no engagement nudges, no upsells. The product's job is to play music and get out of the way.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Keyboard navigation is a first-class concern (the app already has a keyboard shortcuts modal). Reduced motion support via `prefers-reduced-motion`. Sufficient color contrast across all interactive states.
